# Four working days

Four ordinary days in one team, and what each of them taught about working with
an assistant.

---

## Monday: the queue

Elin runs the support inbox. On Monday she pointed an assistant at it and
watched the queue empty for the first time in a year.

+ The assistant read every ticket
+ It drafted every reply
+ Elin approved them in batches of twenty

```@story
Elin, a support engineer, sits at her laptop on the left. A tall inbox stands
at the top centre and an AI orb on the right. Step one: tickets flow from the
inbox into the orb. Step two: drafts flow from the orb back to Elin's laptop.
Step three: the laptop glows as she approves them.
```

## Tuesday: the folder

Jonas, a product owner, dropped a folder of customer interviews into the same
assistant and asked for the themes.

+ It found six themes in eleven minutes
+ Two were things nobody had said out loud
+ One was wrong, and it was the one he liked most

```@story
Jonas at his laptop on the left, a folder of documents centre-top, an AI orb
on the right. Step one: documents stream into the orb. Step two: a report
flows back to the laptop. Step three: one document glows hot, the wrong one.
```

## Wednesday: the gate

Maja, the team lead, put a review gate between the assistant and anything
that left the building.

+ Every outgoing message waited at the gate
+ Money and legal words were flagged automatically
+ Nothing slowed down except the mistakes

```@story
Maja on the left, the AI orb centre-top, a gate centre-bottom, and outgoing
mail on the right. Step one: the orb sends drafts to the gate. Step two: the
gate glows as Maja reviews. Step three: calm white mail leaves the gate.
```

---

## Thursday: the lesson

> The assistant did not change what we do. It changed how often we look.

-- Maja, at Thursday's retrospective

---

## What the week taught

- Point the assistant at the boring part first
- Keep a person at the gate
- Write down what it got wrong, and why you noticed
