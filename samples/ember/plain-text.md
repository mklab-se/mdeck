# Working in the open

How a small team ships software people can trust, one visible decision at a time.

---

## Why visibility beats velocity

Speed without a record is speed you cannot repeat. Every decision worth making
is worth writing down where the next person can find it.

- A decision log beats a meeting nobody remembers
- Pull requests are the documentation you already write
- Public roadmaps set expectations before anyone has to ask

---

## Three habits

+ **Write first.** The message that explains a change is drafted before the change.
+ **Show the seams.** Rough edges are shown early, when they are cheap to fix.
+ **Close the loop.** Every question gets an answer where it was asked.

---

# The cost

---

## What it costs

Working in the open is slower on the first day and faster on every day after.
The first week feels exposed. By the second month, nobody remembers working any
other way.

- Onboarding drops from weeks to days
- Reviews get shorter because context is already written
- Fewer decisions are made twice

---

> The fastest way to find out what you think is to write it down.

-- A colleague, on the first day of a decision log

---

## Where to start

Pick one thing this week and do it in the open.

- Publish the roadmap you already have
- Move one recurring meeting to a written thread
- Write the decision log entry for the choice you made today

# Thank you

Questions are welcome. So are disagreements, as long as they are written down.
